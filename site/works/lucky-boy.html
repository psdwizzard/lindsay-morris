Lucky Boy

by Lindsay Morris

Speculative thriller, 22-minute read

A note before you read: this story may contain strong language, drug use, sexual content, and bodily or psychological distress.

Joey can’t stop pissing himself. That squirming, red-haired motherfucker. When I complain, they give me the heavy chain, shackle me with double-knotted steel cinched tight at my waist. No stretching my legs over the Atlantic now.

I lay my elbows on the stripped plastic armrests and stare at the mini screen cracked into dozens of fresh spiderwebs. The plane has clearly been built for luxury, and despite the guards’ best efforts, it still shows. Some amenities they’d gone out of their way to ruin, like the inflight movies, others they’d simply locked away. Half a dozen of us can’t look away from a bottle of Don Julio sealed behind a glass case a few rows ahead.

When we left Fort Sumter, it was still dark. By the time we boarded in Charleston, the sun was coming up from the east. Hours later it was rising again. I swear we’re flying in circles, pumping and dumping fuel all over the country, before touching down.

“They’re trying to keep it hush-hush,” Joey says.

“What?”

“I don’t know.”

“What don’t you know?”

“Everything.”

We both laugh. I look down at the dark stain drying between his legs and I feel bad for calling him out about the whole pissing-his-pants thing. I hadn’t realized how young he was. No way this rugrat is built for whatever these bastards have in store.

As the hours stretch on, the guards stop bothering to check on us, occasionally tossing packs of crackers to shut up the worst of the complainers. I tear the plastic open and share a square.

“Thanks—”

Joey stalls, eyes flicking to the redacted lanyard shoved around my neck.

“It’s Matterson,” I say.

“Thanks, Mr. Matterson.”

I nod and do my best to get comfortable, ignoring the shifting metal on my groin. None of the seats recline. Someone shoved glue into the little switch by my side. I scratch at it with my nail for a while, trying to get the thing to give, but eventually I give up, cross my arms over my chest, and shut my eyes.

A few hours later I wake up shivering. It was hot when we took off, my pits slick and spicy from the humid Southern air. The guards on the plane are now dressed for much colder weather, outfitted in well-worn blue camo. Some have stripped off their thick fleeces and stretched out; others stayed fully dressed, even keeping their ski masks on.

Tap Tap Tap.

I turn my head and clock Joey smiling.

When he realizes I’m not going to ream him out, he goes back to tapping against the glass, stopping every few seconds to lift his head, looking for one of the guards before starting up again.

Behind me, someone picks it up. Tap Tap Tap.

“Knock it off,” a guard barks, not even looking up.

But then another prisoner joins in. Then another. Fingers on plastic. Nails on glass. The rhythm hopping rows, skipping seats, landing wherever it can. Tap Tap Tap.

“One a penny, two a penny,” I mumble.

Half the plane is in on it, tapping on armrests, trays, windows, knees, anything they can get their hands on, before one of the guards finally stands, kills the lights, and tells us to eat shit and die.

At least the lights are off.

Hours later when they unshackle us, I feel a flicker of relief, a brief burst of hope. Dumbass. The doors are open now and we’re lined up like good little inmates, freezing our asses off while we wait for our voluntary rehabilitation to begin. Snow comes down heavy, big freaking flakes swallowing the ground, making it impossible to get decent leverage as they march us forward, left, right, left, knee to chest, ass to groin, slipping and sliding through the worst of it. I already miss the warm shackles of the plane’s fuselage interior.

Joey is whimpering, cradling his right palm. A piece of skin froze to the metal walkway as we exited the plane and he had to rip it off to keep the line moving.

“My mom didn’t say it was gonna be cold,” he says miserably, a few stray tears already icing beneath his eyes.

I stick my tongue out, guiding it in little waves and divots as I attempt to catch a falling snowflake. A six-sided beauty lands on the tip of my glossa; it tastes like nothing.

Visibility is awful, but even still, it doesn’t take more than a second to look around and know we’ve fucked off to the absolute ass end of the United States, if we’re even still in the US. No laws, no justice. A perfect black hole caked in pretty white snow. I don’t have a clue where we are, but it doesn’t matter. North Dakota or Jupiter, either way, Lady Liberty, that green-eyed bitch wasn't looking.

Most of us do our best to hustle forward until the line comes into view. Other planes must have landed before us. Dozens of inmates stretch out as far as the eye can see, the line bending around a small wooden shack where a few guards stand clustered together, cigarette smoke misting above their heads, before looping back toward the building.

Past them, against the bone-white mountains, stands a hulking mass of cold steel and industry, its bottom half swallowed by rock and snow. Above it, an oblong metal cap bulges with a single long window wrapping around it like a visor. At its base is a fat gray door; in the distance, I can spot a series of red and green lights winking on and off as other inmates step off the line and dart inside.

“I gotta say, zero points for creativity.” The whole thing screams Secret Government Facility.

“Is that where they're gonna do it?” Joey asks, not taking his eyes off of the metal dome.

“Yep. That's where they’re gonna do it.”

Joey finally shuts up, wide eyes flicking up and down before shoving his injured hand into his jumpsuit, cradling it to his chest. We don’t speak for the next little while, inching along minute by minute, all of us shivering, eyeing the guards' cheap wool jackets and padded camo pants with frigid envy. Is there anything worse than bureaucracy in the face of the American death star?

The security guard to my right prowls back and forth, warning us to stay in line. He smokes Pall Malls at a healthy pace, giving us the stink eye after each used butt. It isn’t like anyone is stupid enough to run. We can hardly walk in this shit. If anything, I want to be “rehabilitated” faster if it means getting out of this cold. How quickly resistance dies in zero degree weather. Behind me, one of the less ambitious delinquents is complaining; he griped nonstop on the flight over. I turn to look at the guy. He has close-cropped, freshly buzzed bleach blond hair. His eyebrows are thick black stumps connected by a few stubborn wisps down the middle.

“It’s not fair. I shouldn’t even be here!” he yells over the wind. He kicks at the snow, sending an icy spray back into his own face. “All I did was post a few memes. I wasn’t even the OP.”

“Did you tell them that?” Joey asks, perking up for the first time since we got off the plane.

“Yeah. But those phony cops wouldn’t hear it,” he says. “Since when are memes treason anyway?”

He looks at Joey. “You online a lot?”

Joey shakes his head.

“Figures. I’m kind of famous,” he says. “When we get back, look me up. ZinnWazRight69. I’m the original Milkshake Kid.”

We both shook our heads.

“Come on. You know the one.”

He makes a face, shoves a fist to his chin, arranges his mouth like he’s about to shit.

When we don’t say anything, he keeps going. “They said they already nailed some X user for it. But it was me. I’m telling you.”

He snorts. “There’s records and shit. No way they wipe me completely.”

Joey nods and the guy gives him a rough couple of knuckles to the head. They scuffle a bit, Joey losing his footing, going down twice in the snow before they both come away smiling. ZinnWazRight69 is a full-on jackass, insufferable in a way that would’ve made us friends if we’d met a dozen years ago.

I look around at the lot of us. All men. All assholes. And if you ask me, not one of them deserves whatever’s coming.

But no one asks me.

A few minutes later one of the guys drops down behind us. I see two men propping him up between them, slapping his cheeks, shouting into his face, trying to keep him moving. The line doesn’t stop.

The snow has soaked through our prison shoes, cold seeping up from the soles, numbing ankles, deadening toes. Every step feels heavier than the last. The cold isn’t just on us anymore. It’s inside us, burrowed deep until we’re all shaking and shivering.

Joey starts coughing, choking on phlegm and snot, and ZinnWazRight69’s got an arm around him, giving his back a few hard slaps. “Come on. Get it out.”

“Hey,” he calls out to one of the passing guards. “Can’t you see the kid’s freezing to death?”

The guard doesn’t look back. Just keeps moving.

ZinnWazRight69 watches him go, jaw tight. He snorts, louder now. “Come on, man. I’m freezing my nuts off.”

He grabs his whole package and sort of wangles himself around, gaining a little height in the process. Even in this temperature I catch the faint outline of flesh beneath his thin prison jumpsuit. A few of us laugh. Not because it’s funny, but because it’s better than not laughing.

“You know who I am?” he calls after the guard. “I’m the fucking Milkshake Kid.” He grins.

“You hear me, you fascist fuck?”

The guard, a real thick son of a bitch, yanks him out of line.

“Finally, man,” ZinnWazRight69 says, still smirking. “I’ve been waiting all day.”

The guard throws an elbow into his face.

“Watch his mouth,” another guard says, coming to help as they pull him toward the shack.

“Yeah. Watch my mouth.”

He manages to yank a hand free, spreads a sloppy V with his fingers, sticks his tongue out and rolls it back and forth in fleshy waves.

The other guard gets in a punch to the kidney, and you can hear the breath rattle out of him as they drag him toward the shack, slamming the door shut behind him.

“He’s gonna come back, isn't he?” Joey asks.

“Course he is.” Not a shot.

Another guard wearing a ski mask and visor, bulging and faceless, comes by a minute later handing out juice boxes. He hands me a heavy brick, Pineapple Sunshine, the plastic frozen solid.

Bastard.

I grip it in my hands, flex it between my blue-tipped fingers before shoving it in my pocket.

Joey tries to get his open, but with only one working hand, he’s struggling. His lip snags on the corner where he’s trying to use his teeth and bright red blood blooms fresh on his lip.

My paternal instincts kick in because I grab his juice box instead, wrench the frozen corner loose, and press it into his hand. “Wipe your mouth,” I tell him.

He does, then licks at the edge of the juice, getting a little Sunshine into him, his eyes going wide as the sugar hits. His tongue sticks on contact, the pink stretching as he pulls back. He goes in again, breathing hard on the surface to coax a little warmth before trying once more.

We’re getting closer now, playing red light, green light. The snow is no longer big and fluffy; it’s turned mean, frozen pellets buzzing past my ear. A constant heavy static rattling all around me.

“Looks like I’m almost up,” I yell to Joey.

“Don’t leave me,” he says.

“You’ll be fine. Just a few harmless cuts, a little snip-snip, and we’ll be back on that plane before you know it.” I try to mimic a pair of scissors but my fingers are too numb to make it look right.

I turn to go through but he clings to my arm. I grab his hand, give it a squeeze, then lift it off.

“Try not to piss yourself again, kid,” I say, and rush through as soon as the door slides open.

My hands fly up on instinct, palms pressed hard over my eyes, my whole body convulsing in sharp, involuntary twitches as the heat of the room thaws me out like frostbitten chicken.

It takes a moment for me to get what's what, but eventually I get myself going. The room is bright, overly large, a mix of metal and blue tile set in dark gray grout covering the walls. The ceiling is gridded with long fluorescent panels sunk into white acoustic plates, muting a faint hum I can feel low in my gut.

“Jamie Matterson?” A middle-aged woman with a fat ass asks.

I nod. She gestures me forward, already moving behind her boat-like metal desk. I step up to the desk, my feet landing on a red X taped to the floor.

“Welcome to the Prosthetic Occlusion Compliance Clinic, Jamie," she says sweetly.

Reperfusion tears through my arms and legs, sharp needles bursting into cherry red flames as circulation staggers back. I shift my weight, hunting for a scrap of relief. My right leg bounces without permission, jittering up and down as the cold drains out of it.

“Please stand still while our computer finishes its scans.”

“And if I don’t?” I say. “If I tell you to go fuck yourself after keeping me out in the cold all night?”

The woman behind the desk smiles a little wider. “If you don’t do what you’re told, Jamie, I’ll have one of the guards come in, break your legs, and hold you up until I’m finished with you.”

I give her a little fuck you salute and stand still.

“No defects, abnormalities, or signs of cancerous rot,” she says. “Aren’t you the healthy boy.”

“You’re coming on a bit strong, aren't you?” I say, forcing a wink. “What time do you get off tonight?”

She ignores me and puts a fresh tablet on the desk.

“Sign here.” The tablet lights up, revealing bright white disclaimers in bold black lettering. There are hundreds of pages in the terms and conditions for me to read. I try to scan the first few paragraphs but after a minute I give up, scroll to the bottom, and sign.

I know a bullshit deal when I see one, but what are you gonna do? They had me dead to rights.

“Come on now,” I say. “I’m a great lay. Ask my ex, Kiko. She said the only thing wrong with me was my shitty personality. Not a word against my pecker, I swear.”

“We also need verbal consent. Please turn your head towards the camera,” she says icily, that pretty little smile turning sour.

“Which one?” I look around; the place is covered with them. Mounted in the corners of the ceiling, bolted onto scanning machines. Hell, there was even one dangling from the chest of fat ass right above her security clearance badge.

“Pick one,” she says, annoyed.

I choose tits.

“Do you agree to allow us to remove your molars, incisors, front teeth, back teeth, gum line, and jaw bone if necessary?”

I sort of wish I hadn't skipped those pages.

I nod, then remember myself. “Yes. Only if necessary? Right?”

“Next,” the woman calls, and I am already being corralled down a long, millennium gray hallway by one of the nameless camo boys. Now that the hour is actually upon me, I am no longer so keen on the deal after all.

My hand slips into my pocket and finds the juice box, still half frozen, slick with condensation. My fingers tighten around Pineapple Sunshine.

“Hey,” I say, turning just enough to catch the guard’s attention.

I bring the juice box down hard. The carton connects with the top of his skull. The sound is ugly and wet, like striking rain-soaked wood. He stumbles forward and I am grateful for the anonymity of the ski mask. I do not want to know his face.

I turn and run, barreling down the hall. Locked doors on all sides. I grab handles at random and I can smell my fear sweating through my jumpsuit. This was a really fucking dumb idea.

It occurs to me, too late, that the guard might have had keys. Or a security badge or a fucking phone.

I double back and find him no longer crumpled on the floor, but on his hands and knees, already pushing himself upright.

“You motherfucker,” he says, rubbing the top of his head.

I drop to the floor and reach for his pockets as he struggles to stand. It's awkward, all elbows and knees, the two of us tangled on the floor like idiots.

“I don’t have anything,” he snaps, shoving me off. “They don’t give us shit.”

We end up sitting there for a second, backs against the wall, knees up. He keeps rubbing his head and I keep breathing too fast.

“You got two choices,” he says finally. “Go where I tell you, or get us both in trouble and go right down the fucking mountain.”

“I’ll take the first one.”

He gets to his feet, hauls me up by the arm, walks ten feet, gut punches me, and shoves me through the nearest open door.

“Jesus,” I hear him mutter as it slams shut. “I hate this job.”

It doesn't look like any dentist’s office I’ve ever seen. I thought the equipment would be silver. I thought it would smell like fluoride and root beer. Goddamnit. I even let myself hope for those little lollipops they hand you after they floss your teeth.

Everything is wrong. From the uniformed grey walls to the chair with its thick metal restraints and the sour smell of ammonia permeating the air.

Fifteen minutes of freedom. Those first sweet steps of patriotic duty, and this is the reward.

It hardly seems fair.

But nothing has been fair in a long time. Not work. Not life, and certainly not the government.

I don’t even know what the hell came over me. One minute I’m rushing home to make dinner, trying to decide if it’s worth texting Kiko again or if I should just throw in the towel, and the next thing I know some Steely Dan motherfucker hands me a sign and I’m chanting with all the rest of them, fist high above me.

All around me were sweaty faces, sun-blotched skin, bony arms, pit-stained shirts. Bodies pressed close as we marched and sang, spitting out the furious taste of a system built against us. I was. We were. In sync.

I didn't even realize until we were half way down First Ave that I was marching for anybody other than myself. That maybe it wasn't just me that was sick of getting reamed out by my manager and her manager and all the managers before her.

It wasn't my fault someone hoisted me on their shoulders. It wasn't my fault that it felt so damn good. Fifteen minutes later a policeman had a boot on my neck and he didn't want to hear it wasn't my fault. I was arrested for the first time in my life. Never in my wildest fucking nightmares did I think it could happen to someone like me. What can I say? I thought I was immune. Exempt. Free and clear from any and all police brutality. They charged me with so many crimes it was separated by a second page break.

I have never felt such fear. Such cold, naked terror.

Then, beneath the words fifteen to life and Treason with a capital T, was the little check box that opted out of a trial in exchange for “experimental rehabilitation.”

It wasn't fair, and somehow it still surprised me I ever thought it would be.

I lie back in the chair. Why fight it?

The dentist comes in wearing a gray mask and matching scrubs. His eyes are bright blue. Cerulean, my mother would have said. She loved colors and hated politics.

She doesn’t know I’m here. Never even got the chance to break her heart. One phone call my ass.

“Do you guys ever think of choosing another color? All this metal on metal shit is really ahhhh.”

It hits my sinuses before I can process what happened, the spray, the mace, whatever the fuck it is the bastard just held up to my face, immediately gets absorbed by all those microscopic blood vessels in my mouth.

I gag a few times. I feel my whole body convulse, swallowing itself bit by bit until eventually I’m spit out again, a jellied wet mass.

“How about a little warning next time?” I sit up, wipe my face clumsily, my jaw already going numb.

“There won't be a next time, Jamie. Please lay down,” He pushes my head until it hits the headrest.

The lights dim. “Was that real, or was I just imagining it?”

He winks at me, then activates the restraints on the table. My arms become immobile. Not that I could move them anyway.

“Relax, Jamie.”

But I can’t relax. And I hate that he keeps using my name. We aren’t buddies, he isn’t someone I want with their fingers all up in my mucosa.

The doctor sticks something in my mouth. My tongue gets a taste of copper, bright fresh pennies dripping down my throat as my lips stretch wider and wider. He sticks a gloved finger inside me, tickling my uvula, and I can feel spittle beginning to flow down my chin.

“I want to goh fhomme,” I say. I can't help it.

“You’ve got a beautiful set of incisors.” I hear the clink clink as he taps them with professional admiration. Feel the vibration in my collarbone.

“Some of these boys who come in. No standards. Mouth full of cavities.”

He wrinkles his nose. “But every once in a while I get a beautiful set to work with. Just the other day I had a boy on my table with the most handsome molars. Broke my heart, ripping them out.”

He turns and uncovers a tray. I get a glimpse of dark titanium wires, thick copper filament, tiny Phillips-head screws. Nasty pieces of hardware laid out in rows. None of it gleaming. None of it new and none of it looks like it belongs anywhere near my mouth.

“You have no idea what a lucky boy you are,” he says, grabbing a pair of pliers off the tray. “This is far more humane than what we used to do.”

He shoves the pliers into the back of my mouth. I feel a sharp pressure, then an impossible pull, something tears. He sticks his hand back into my mouth and a moment later comes away satisfied, a nerve, dangling and wiggly like a worm, between his fingers.

I imagine the pain was excruciating, not sure how some of them managed it. But don’t worry, we learned the hard way lithium batteries and flesh just don’t mix.”

I am terrified and I don't believe for a minute all the little lies the drugs are telling my nerve endings.

My eyes flicker back to the doctor’s baby blues.

He pulls a light down from above my head and I try to turn away, but it shines so bright I think I’ve gone blind, and without ever choosing it, the darkness carries me away.

I wake up in stages. My head is pounding and my ass aches from whatever shitty seat I’ve been shoved into.

My eyes open to a large sliver of daylight. I’m in a window seat, back on the plane. From what I can tell, Good Old America is staring back at me through the glass to my right. Whether it’s the same one I left, I couldn’t say.

All around me are wrapped faces. Bandaged jaws. Shirts soaked with drool, laps heavy with bloody cotton. I feel something wet and warm slide down the back of my neck.

My hand moves clumsily to my head and come away holding a lukewarm bag I assume was once filled with ice.

I reach up to touch my face and it feels numb. My fingers trace the swollen parts of my jaw.

His eyes seemed cloudy and panicked under a shock of distinctive copper eyebrows. All the more gruesome against a crown of white gauze.

“Okay, patriots. Listen up.” I crane my neck and lift up in my seat.

The guard at the front of the plane has his mask off. He looks early fifties. His army wannabe crew cut can’t disguise the balding at his temples. His posture is unnatural, a cheap imitation of a German shepherd held at attention.

“I said wake up.”

He kicks the back of a seat with his heavy black boots. The man in it lurches forward, still foggy.

“You’ve all been rehabilitated at the great expense of the United States of America.”

He claps once, then walks slowly down the aisle, his hand dragging over the gauze of a few shaved heads.

“Thanks to science, or engineering, or whatever the hell it was, Lady Liberty will be shining her satellite on you, twenty-four-seven. That means no more nasty words out of those mangled mouths of yours.” He stops and presses a finger to Joey’s forehead, right between the eyes.

“Nnnoh wuhh stannn… alooonnn,” Joey slurs.

From behind him I hear someone else pick it up. “Nooh wuhh sthan alohn.”

More voices join in as it moves like a virus through the cabin, skipping rows, infecting bodies. Accents blurr, tempos falling in line. Getting louder and louder until it's all I can hear.

Over and over, the words ooze past swollen gums and novocained tongues, each syllable stumbling out wet and thick, gaining strength in numbers as our voices become indistinguishable in the chorus of patriotism.

“NO ONE STANDS ALONE!” We are in sync. A single organism throbbing under one patriotic pulse.

To the left of me, Joey’s jaw moves faster, his teeth slamming into one another as he fights to control his movements. Enamel cracks against enamel, revealing the wet pulp underneath as horror shines through his drug addled eyes. He shoves his fingers in his mouth, tugging and frothing as bits of bone and blood speckle my face.

I grab at his hands, trying to stop him, but he turns his panic on me. Joey grabs my throat. I can feel the dry, cracked edges of his fingernails pressing into the soft flesh of my Adam’s apple.

A hot blast of foul, heavy rot from his ruined teeth nearly overwhelms me before I smash a fist along the side of his face, trying to get free. Pain shoots through my knuckles as they crunch against hard skull.

I watch as the whites of his eyes bloom red with the blow, then black, as every fragile vein bursts at once, flooding the sclera with thick, rust-colored fluid.

The chorus around us grows louder and louder until all I can hear is them, and all I can see is him.

The German shepherd raises his hands, trying to quiet us, but our voices roll over him, louder and louder. He grabs the collar of some poor asshole and slaps twice, trying to make him stop chanting. It doesn't do a damn thing. His face reddens as he shouts for backup, and I look back at Joey.

I want to tell him it will all be okay. That we can fix what they have broken. That he should join ZinnWazRight69 and start shitposting on Reddit. That memes are his goddamn right as an American.

But instead I bear hug him, trying to ignore the mechanical movements of his jaw against my shoulder. Wishing I could say something, anything, but my own mouth keeps the chant going.

Tap Tap Tap. It’s the only thing I can do.

Tap Tap Tap.

I hold him close, tap tap tapping on his skull until his jaw slows and he finally shuts up.
